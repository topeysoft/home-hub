One cloud, every condition, both lights

Not thirty drawings — one illustration built from parts, driven by the same {clouds, rain, snow, fog, lightning, wind} that wxOf() already hands the sky. Night is not one of those parts, and it is not a condition: the house reports the weather, the sun’s elevation says which light it is in. So every condition has to hold up twice. Day on the left of each pair, night on the right. Only the clear sky carries two names.

What changes between a pair: the sky itself, by palette()’s own rules — cloud greys it, rain darkens it, fog flattens it, in either light; the disc, a gold sun or a bitten silver moon; the stars a thin enough sky lets through; and the cloud body, lit white by day, only moonlit by night. Nothing else moves. The bolt is the same colour in both, because lightning brings its own light.
