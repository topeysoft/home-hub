For

The wait costs nothing at all — no traffic, no buffers, no MQTT, and the mesh gets its rate back while somebody walks to the strip. The notification is still ciphertext the puck cannot read, so the tube is as sealed as it ever was, and an adoption becomes seven exchanges, well inside even the stock buffer pool.

Against

It needs firmware on the strip, which one already in a house will not have — so the hub needs a fallback, and there are two ways of waiting rather than one. It also makes the courier carry something unsolicited, the first thing here that is not a reply, and a dropped notification is a household at a strip that says nothing. A timeout has to sit behind it.
